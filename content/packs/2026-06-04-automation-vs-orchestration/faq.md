# FAQ — Automation vs Orchestration

## What is the difference between automation and orchestration?

**Automation** completes a defined task when an event fires in a system—copy data, send a notification, run a script, route a row. **Orchestration** coordinates timing, ownership, and outcomes **across** systems and teams so the business delivers a full performance—not isolated steps. Automation is a measure in the score; orchestration is the score and the conductor.

## Why do automations work but the business still feels chaotic?

Tasks complete in silos while **journeys** lack owners and visible failure across handoffs. Sales, operations, finance, and AI each play alone without shared sheet music. The fix is coordinated rules, observability on outcomes, and conductor ownership—not more solo triggers.

## Do I need more Zaps or better orchestration?

Usually neither first. You need journey clarity on one critical path, stable manual process where humans still judge, and sheet music everyone follows. Tools are musicians; another Zap without coordination adds noise. Place working automations inside a governed journey before buying more connectors.

## Is orchestration just fancy integration?

No. Integration moves data between systems; orchestration owns **performance**—shared rules, human checkpoints, observability, escalation, and accountability when something breaks. Pipes without a conductor produce movement, not clarity operators can trust.

## What is the orchestration layer in business operations?

The **orchestration layer** is the coordination discipline between tools, workflows, data, and AI performers. It aligns musicians (systems), sheet music (routing and approvals), performers (AI in roles), and the orchestra pit (permissioned data) under conductor ownership. It is not a feature inside one app—it is how the business performs as one.

## Where does AI fit—automation or orchestration?

AI is a **performer** in defined roles—qualify, summarize, route, report—not a substitute for orchestration. Without sheet music and permissioned pit access, AI amplifies noise: fast answers that route nowhere governed. Performers still need a conductor and a score.

## What is the cornerstone distinction leaders should remember?

**Automation = task. Orchestration = coordination across systems.** Faster fragments do not equal a business that performs as one. Celebrate task wins; do not mistake them for journey performance.

## How do I know if we have an orchestration gap?

Look for manual re-entry at handoffs, per-tool ownership instead of journey ownership, leadership unable to see stalls, AI blocked for ungoverned access, and a growing automation count with the same customer pain. Three or more signals on a revenue-adjacent journey mean coordination gap—not tool shortage.

## What breaks when orchestration is missing?

Handoffs fail silently, visibility disappears between systems, AI guesses without context, and leadership reviews automation counts instead of journey outcomes. Customers experience improvisation even when every webhook returns success in its own tool.

## Can we keep existing automations?

Yes. Orchestration wraps working task automations in governed journeys with owners and cross-system observability—so they serve outcomes, not a pile of solo triggers. You rarely need to rip and replace; you need sheet music and a conductor.

## What does good orchestration look like in practice?

Operators feel calm because work moves with clarity, coordinated timing, performance measured on whole journeys, visible recoverable failure on critical paths, and human checkpoints where judgment matters. Reliability beats a fast fragile chain of tasks.

## When is a task automation enough?

Some work is genuinely local: format a notification, copy a field, archive a row. Keep task automations when they sit inside clear boundaries, have a builder-owner, and do not pretend to own a customer outcome. The mistake is treating a pile of local wins as proof the business performs end to end.
