02 · Des · Front desk

Lead in once. Schedule from one calendar.

Des

Lead intake

  • One place to enter the lead — name, source, Community-style status, designer.
  • New Event for the design appointment. No second copy of the same consult.
  • Notes / chatter stay on the lead so the next person is not guessing.

When it sells

  • Sold intake captures contract $, deposit, Studio/Community refs, job-check owner.
  • That creates the job — the record everyone else will hang work on.
  • Clear next step: mark the 50% in Billing, then it shows in Ready to Schedule.

Installs (her daily desk)

  • Today strip: awaiting deposit · ready to schedule · unassigned this week.
  • Week calendar is first. Sold/ready queues sit behind a toggle so they don’t bury the grid.
  • 50% deposit is required before an install can be saved.
  • Job check → Ordered → receive date is the path; the OS warns if receive date is empty (does not trap her).
  • When she schedules: Slack fires. She logs “install confirm sent” after she texts/calls the client.

Her path, in one line

Lead → design appointment → sold intake → deposit in Billing → Ready → job check / receive → drop on the Install Calendar → confirm the client.

03 · Craig · Payroll & sales

The red workbook, without living in Excel.

Craig

Craig’s muscle memory stays. Contract, deposit, starting margin, after-spiff, final margin, commission %, check, pay date — same columns, now rows in the OS.

Payroll desk

  • Sync from the imported workbook creates jobs + clients so the rest of the OS isn’t empty.
  • He can still work a row the way he always did — the OS just stops being a sidecar to Excel.
  • 45% gate is visible before a check / spiff is treated as payable.

Leads vs Sales

  • Designer board: who was assigned vs who actually converted.
  • Closing ratio per designer, on the same jobs Des is scheduling.
  • No separate “Craig spreadsheet vs Des calendar” version of the truth.

Once a workbook row is a job, Des can schedule it, Frank can hang parts on it, and Lulu can cost it. That is the point of bringing Excel in — not prettier cells.

04 · Lulu · Billing & finance

50 / 40 / 10, then the real job cost.

Lulu

Billing

  • Every sold job gets deposit / 40% at install / 10% final.
  • Des records that the customer paid (Podium / check). Lulu owns the books.
  • QuickBooks stays the books. The OS never writes into QB.
  • After she enters it in QB, she taps Marked in QB — same idea as checking it off in Excel.

One job cost card

  • Materials — pulled from parts allocated to that job (not a guess).
  • Labor — from installer clock-ins, or an override.
  • Fees — plus Stow / pallet / freight lines (the hidden $200 pallet).
  • Commission + spiff. Margin vs the 45% gate. Mark costs verified when the Stow invoice is itemized.

What lights up for her

Who still owes · payments not yet entered in QB · jobs below the spiff gate · costs still unverified. That’s the daily list — not a hunt through tabs.

05 · Frank & Brian · Inventory

Know what’s on the shelf before you delete it off the order.

Frank · Brian

The undermounted-slides job is the problem this desk exists to stop: Brian thought we had them (wrong size). Frank deleted them off the Stow order. Customer paid in full. A week later, still waiting.

What’s already live

  • Parts catalog: SKU, name, bin, on-hand, reserved, reorder point, $ value.
  • Movement history on every part — receive, allocate, return, adjust, scrap.
  • Allocate a part to a job so materials $ shows on Jobs, Ready, and Finance.
  • Attention strip: low stock · excess $ · receives with no allocate · installs with $0 materials.

The two habits after the count

1. Receive when a pallet / Stow order lands. Stock goes up.

2. Allocate (or reserve at job check) when parts are promised to a job. Available stock goes down.

That’s it. Counts, value, low-stock flags, and job cost fall out of those two buttons.

The deal for this meeting: Brian/Frank do a one-time upload of what’s actually in the warehouse. After that they only log receives and allocates. A ledger nobody keeps current is just Brian’s memory with a login.

06 · Installers & Gavin

Field feeds labor. Gavin sees the shop.

Installers

Field app

  • Clock in / out on the job. Those hours flow into Lulu’s labor cost — no extra timesheet.
  • Photos and issue flags (missing part, damage, site not ready) sit on the same job Des scheduled.

Gavin

Visibility without Excel

  • Dashboard: today’s attention, margins, cash, who still owes.
  • Cubby: ask in plain language — “what’s leaking?”, “who’s below the gate?”
  • Inventory attention is the warehouse version of the same idea: low stock, excess dollars, jobs that went to install with no materials tied on.

Why this is one system, not six apps

Des’s calendar, Frank’s parts, Lulu’s margin, and Gavin’s attention are looking at the same job. If inventory isn’t on the job, the schedule is guessing and the margin is lying. That’s why the next slice is not “more screens” — it’s making stock and schedule talk to each other.

08 · What’s next — decisions for this meeting

Make stock real. Then let the calendar think.

Built next, in this order. Each one is useless without the one before it.

1 One-time warehouse upload

Brian (or Frank) counts what’s on the shelf. We import it — SKU, size, bin, qty, cost. Size is its own field so “undermount slide” cannot silently mean the wrong length.

2 Reserve at job check

When Frank checks a job, he picks what comes from stock vs. what must be ordered. If available qty is short, the OS stops him from pretending we have it. That’s the slides story, caught at order time — not install day.

3 Stage / pack / damaged

Brian ticks each reserved part as pulled and loaded. Damaged → scrap + “need a replacement?” Installs in the next 3 days with unstaged parts light up on attention.

4 Crew size + how long the job takes

Frank/Des enter guys needed and estimated days on the same job-check screen. Receive date already exists. Without these, no scheduler has anything to work with.

5 Suggest the calendar — Des confirms

The OS drafts installs, services, and go-backs around arrival dates and crew capacity. Nothing auto-books. Des reviews, adjusts, then calls every client on the list — exactly as she described.

Questions for Gavin

1. Will Frank and Brian own a one-time count + upload, then receive/allocate as the only daily inventory habit?

2. Is job check the moment we freeze “from stock vs. order” — even if it adds 2 minutes per job?

3. Does Des stay the human who confirms clients, with the OS only proposing days — not texting customers itself yet?
