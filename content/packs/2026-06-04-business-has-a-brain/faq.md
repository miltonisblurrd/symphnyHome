# FAQ — Business Has a Brain

## What is a business brain vs a chatbot?

A **chatbot** optimizes conversation; a **business brain** answers from governed live context—permissions, workflows, audit—with pause and escalate when truth is unavailable. Brains serve operations; chatbots serve chat volume. The test is behavior under hard questions, not tone.

## What is a "business brain"?

A **business brain** is governed orchestration that lets AI use real business context safely—not a generic assistant on stale documents. It includes sheet music (what AI may do per stage), the orchestra pit (permissioned data), and conductor ownership (ongoing tuning).

## What does Symphony MCP do for operations?

Symphony MCP connects AI **performers** to the orchestra pit—current services, philosophy, FAQs, case patterns—with visible tool use and explicit limits. Buyers care about **behavior** (grounded answers, escalation, pause rules), not protocol slides or repository tours.

## What is MCP in plain language for operators?

MCP is the **orchestra pit**—the governed layer where AI may read permitted business context and show what it checked. Operators judge whether answers match CRM, pricing, and policy—not whether developers used a particular protocol.

## How do I evaluate AI that knows our business?

Test the top ten real operator questions; score accuracy, currency, permissions, and honest limits—penalize fluent wrong answers. Require logs: who asked, tools run, data touched. Demos should show behavior, not repositories.

## Why do internal AI assistants hallucinate company facts?

They lack governed pit access—relying on aging exports or hero prompts. Truth diverges from CRM, pricing, and policy. Fix coordination: permissions, audit, conductor updates—not a larger model alone.

## What is governed access for AI in enterprise?

Role-based boundaries, audit on sensitive paths, human approval for exceptions, and revocable access when teams or policies change. The pit prevents performers from improvising around failed upstream steps or silent data gaps.

## Is MCP the product?

No. MCP is pit infrastructure. Symphony Studio **orchestrates** the whole performance—tools, sheet music, performers, and conductor ownership over time. You buy coordination, not a chat SKU.

## How is MCP different from RAG on documents?

Documents age and lack permissions. Orchestration connects live rules and systems with a conductor maintaining truth. RAG can be one pit source—not the whole brain. MCP behavior should show which permitted sources ran, not just retrieved chunks.

## Do we need developers or operators to trust a business brain?

Implementation may involve technical setup; **trust** is operational. Operators judge grounding, limits, and escalation. Developers wire the pit. Leadership should sign off on behavior specs and access matrices—not SDK choices.

## What should leadership see in a demo?

Questions → tool use → grounded answers → human escalation paths—not model benchmarks or code walks first. If the vendor cannot repeat your ops team's question live, treat the pilot as chatbot-grade.

## What breaks without an orchestra pit for AI?

Silent wrong answers, compliance exposure, trust collapse, security blocking production, and hero prompt dependency. Performers guess when the pit is down instead of pausing visibly.

## How is orchestration different from MCP?

MCP is the pit—permissioned access and visible tool behavior. **Orchestration** is the conductor plus sheet music plus failure contracts across the stack. Pit without conductor goes stale; conductor without pit has nothing trustworthy to tune.

## Can a business brain replace our stack?

No. It coordinates the stack you have—CRM, ticketing, finance, internal databases—as musicians under sheet music. Brains add governed context and journey behavior; they do not rip out systems that already work.
